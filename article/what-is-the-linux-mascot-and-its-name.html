What Is the Linux Mascot and Its Name?

The official mascot of the Linux operating system is a plump, friendly penguin named Tux. Chosen by Linux creator Linus Torvalds in the mid-1990s, this iconic flightless bird has become one of the most recognizable symbols in the tech world. This article explores the identity of the Linux mascot, the story behind why a penguin was selected, how Tux got his name, and the mascot's enduring legacy in the open-source community.

The Mascot: Tux the Penguin

The Linux mascot is an anthropomorphic penguin named Tux. Unlike corporate logos that are rigid and strictly trademarked, Tux was designed to represent the approachable, community-driven spirit of open-source software. He is traditionally depicted sitting down, resting after a meal, with a calm and contented smile.

Why a Penguin?

The decision to make a penguin the face of Linux came directly from Linus Torvalds, the creator of the Linux kernel. During discussions in 1996 regarding an official logo or mascot for the operating system, several animals and abstract concepts were suggested. Torvalds expressed a strong preference for penguins, partially inspired by a visit to the National Zoo & Aquarium in Canberra, Australia, where he was playfully nipped by a small fairy penguin. Torvalds wanted a mascot that was friendly, memorable, and fun rather than a formal corporate icon.

Creation and Design

The visual design of Tux was created by programmer and artist Larry Ewing in 1996. Ewing drew the original illustration using GIMP (GNU Image Manipulation Program), a free and open-source raster graphics editor. The image was entered into a Linux logo contest and quickly became the definitive representation of the operating system. Because Ewing released the artwork under terms that allowed anyone to use and modify it as long as they credited him, Tux was readily adopted by developers worldwide.

The Origin of the Name "Tux"

The name "Tux" was coined by developer James Hughes. It serves as an abbreviation for "tuxedo," referencing the black-and-white plumage of penguins that resembles formal evening wear. Additionally, Hughes noted that it serves as an acronym derived from Torvalds’ UniX.

Tux's Role Today

Tux is a universal symbol for Linux distributions, software, and open-source culture. Beyond documentation and operating system installers, Tux appears prominently in community projects and video games, such as Tux Racer and SuperTux. Today, Tux remains a beloved figure representing freedom, collaboration, and alternative computing.
